Direct revenue

The commission maths most hotels never run

A 15% OTA cut sounds survivable until you put it next to your actual margin rather than your rate.

12 Aug 20265 min read

Ask a hotelier what an OTA costs them and you will usually hear a percentage. Fifteen, eighteen, twenty-two. It sounds like a marketing expense, and marketing expenses feel negotiable.

The number that matters is not the percentage of the rate. It is the percentage of the margin.

A worked example

Take a room at €180 a night. Housekeeping, laundry, amenities, utilities and the front-desk time attached to that stay come to roughly €95. Your contribution margin is €85.

Sell it through an OTA at 18% and you hand over €32.40. That is not 18% of anything you get to keep — it is 38% of your margin on that room. Two rooms sold through an OTA earn you roughly what one direct booking does.

The commission is a percentage of the rate but a much larger percentage of the profit. Those two numbers are not close to each other.

Why the channel mix compounds

It gets worse over a year, because the mix is rarely stable. Every OTA booking teaches the guest that the OTA is where your hotel lives. The next stay starts there too, and so does the one after that. You are renting a relationship you already own.

Run the same maths across a season. A ninety-room property at 70% occupancy with half its bookings coming through OTAs is giving away a six-figure sum in margin — not revenue, margin — to a channel that is showing the guest your competitors on the same page.

What actually moves the number

  • Rate parity, honestly applied. If your own site is never cheaper, there is no reason to book there. A modest direct-only benefit is enough.
  • A booking flow that finishes. Guests who arrive from an OTA already know your property. Losing them at checkout is the expensive failure.
  • Search visibility for your own name. If an OTA outranks you for your own hotel, you are paying commission on traffic that was looking for you.

None of this removes OTAs from the mix. They are genuinely good at discovery. The goal is to stop paying discovery rates on guests who had already found you.
