Booking flow

Where bookings leak out of your checkout

Guests rarely abandon because of price. They abandon because of a surprise, a re-entry, or a form that forgot what they typed.

3 Jul 20266 min read

Cart abandonment is a retail word and it fits badly here. A guest who leaves your booking flow has usually not decided against your hotel. They have decided against your form.

The four common leaks

The surprise total. A rate is shown for four screens, then a city tax, a cleaning fee and a card surcharge appear at the last step. The number is defensible. The timing is not. Show the total early, even when it is higher — guests forgive a price far more readily than they forgive a reveal.

The re-entry. The guest picked dates on the homepage. The booking engine loads and asks for dates again. It is a small thing that says, clearly, that these are two different systems held together with tape.

The forced account. Requiring registration before checkout costs more bookings than the loyalty data is worth. Take the booking, then offer the account on the confirmation screen, when the guest has a reason to say yes.

The silent mobile failure. A date picker that opens off-screen, a total that sits below the fold, a keyboard covering the submit button. These almost never show up in desk-based testing and they are where the majority of traffic actually is.

How to find yours

You do not need a research budget. You need three things:

  • Funnel events on every step, so you can see which screen loses people rather than guessing.
  • Session recordings on mobile only, watched for twenty minutes. This is uncomfortable and extremely fast.
  • One real booking a month, made by you, on your own phone, paying with your own card, all the way to the confirmation email.
Most booking flows are tested by people who already know where the buttons are. That is the one group whose experience does not matter.

Fix the surprise first. It is the leak that costs you the guests who were furthest along and most ready to pay.
