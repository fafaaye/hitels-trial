Performance

Speed is a booking feature, not a tech detail

Every second your room page takes to load costs conversions, and hotel sites are unusually good at being slow.

29 Jul 20264 min read

Page speed gets filed under engineering, which is why it rarely gets fixed. It belongs under revenue.

A guest comparing three properties on a phone, on hotel wifi, in an airport, is not going to wait. They will not tell you they left. They will simply be counted as traffic that did not convert, and the report will say the rate was wrong.

What actually makes hotel sites slow

In our experience it is almost never the framework. It is four things, in roughly this order:

  • Unoptimised photography. Hotels have beautiful images and upload them at full camera resolution. A 4,000-pixel-wide JPEG displayed in a 400-pixel card is thirty times more data than the guest needs.
  • A booking widget loaded on every page. Third-party engines often ship a large script sitewide, including on pages where nobody is booking.
  • Tag managers with archaeology in them. Every pixel from every campaign since 2019, still firing.
  • Fonts that block rendering. Four weights of two families, loaded before anything paints.

What to fix first

Resize the images. It is unglamorous and it is usually the single biggest win available — often a majority of total page weight, fixable in an afternoon, with no visible change to the design.

If you do one thing this quarter, serve your photography at the size it is actually displayed. Nothing else on this list comes close.

Then defer the booking script until the guest signals intent, audit the tag manager with a willingness to delete, and cut the font loading to the weights you genuinely use.

The measure that matters is not a score out of a hundred. It is how long a real guest on a real phone waits before they can see a room and press a button.
